Sergey Alexeev Proposed project

HOPE

Health Outcomes and Post-incarceration Evaluation

HOPE is a proposed Australian research project on how imprisonment affects health, comparing people sent to prison with people given community orders.

Proposal Feedback

Question

The project asks what happens to people's health after prison, and whether the health effects differ from outcomes after community-based orders.

It is designed to produce clear, decision-facing evidence for justice and health policy in Australia, with particular attention to outcomes for Aboriginal people and communities.

Approach

HOPE compares people who receive custodial sentences with similar people who receive community orders. The goal is to estimate the effect of imprisonment itself, rather than simply describing the health profile of people who enter prison.

Population People moving through Australian justice pathways.
Comparison Custodial sentences and community orders.
Purpose Evidence for health, justice, and reintegration policy.

Why It Matters

Prison can change access to care, continuity of treatment, mental health, substance use risks, housing stability, employment, and family life. Those pathways are deeply connected, so the project treats health after imprisonment as both a clinical and social policy question.

By working with Aboriginal leaders and people with lived experience, HOPE aims to make the research more accountable to the communities most affected by incarceration.

Outputs

  • Policy-ready estimates of the health effects of imprisonment.
  • Plain-language summaries for partners, communities, and policymakers.
  • Academic papers on post-incarceration health and causal evaluation in justice settings.
