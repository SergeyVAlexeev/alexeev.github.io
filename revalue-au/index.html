REVALUE-AU

Revaluing Work and Evaluating Labour-market Adjustment in Australia

When the law re-prices work, what does the labour market do? REVALUE-AU will study what happens after tribunals recognise that care and community work has been undervalued — from wages and hours to retention, mobility, employers, funders and service delivery.

Led by Sergey Alexeev, in collaboration with Robert Breunig and Nathan Deutscher. An ARC Discovery proposal is in development.

The problem

A tribunal can re-set the value of work. It cannot dictate what follows.

Australian wage-setting decisions have recognised that feminised care and community work was historically undervalued. The legal record is detailed. The labour-market record — what changed for workers, employers, funders and services afterwards — is much harder to see.

The central gap: a wage order is a legal event. Its consequences are an economic and institutional sequence.

Common surveys can show wages, hours and employment, but they do not directly observe award coverage, classifications, pay points, over-award margins, funding exposure or whether an employer absorbed, passed on or resisted a new wage floor. REVALUE-AU makes those connections explicit.

The programme spine

An evidence platform, not a catalogue of wage cases.

Each institutional intervention is treated as a traceable sequence: what was decided, who was exposed, when the change became operative, and which outcomes could plausibly move next.

01 · Legal event

Build a verified record of decisions, coverage, quantum, staging, operative dates and the mechanism of change.

02 · Implemented treatment

Translate legal language into transparent exposure measures for workers, occupations, sectors, employers and funders.

03 · Realised adjustment

Track wages, hours, employment, retention, mobility and the organisational responses around a re-priced job.

04 · Service consequences

Ask whether changes in labour costs and workforce stability alter capacity, quality, access and the distribution of care.

Every matter enters the programme with an assigned role, and enters the empirical core only once its implementing instrument, operative date and classification schedule are verified.

2005–2009State validation cases
Queensland pay-equity matters in dental assisting, children's services, community services and disability support: the pre-federal lineage, and the basis for state-based comparison designs.
2012–2020Principal anchor
The national Social, Community and Disability Services Equal Remuneration Order, delivered in annual instalments to 2020: the best-documented historical event, and the case the pilot tests.
2018–2021Method benchmarks
Pharmacy and teachers work-value decisions, where the reasoning on properly fixed minimum rates and benchmark classifications was developed. Institutional comparators, not yet coded empirical shocks.
2022–2024Contemporary anchor
Aged care work value, including an interim increase for specified direct-care classifications: institutionally central, politically live, and the strongest case for higher-frequency data.
2024–2025Live implementation
The priority-awards review, reaching dental assistants and related feminised classifications. Revaluation as it happens, with final determinations and operative arrangements still to be established.

Pilot evidence

The pilot changes the question from “did wages rise?” to “what can these data establish?”

The initial HILDA-based study uses the SACS sequence to test the measurement and design problem before the broader platform is built. It is deliberately cautious: the pilot is a feasibility and evidence-mapping exercise, not a claim that one observational estimate settles the causal question.

≈ 9%

The smallest average wage change the pilot design can reliably detect: about 0.09 log points. Many plausible partial pass-through scenarios sit below that threshold, so a household panel can be consistent with a real wage effect and with none. The pooled estimate itself is small and imprecise, and is reported with its confidence interval in the paper. That is the case for making exposure measurement, rather than estimation alone, the centre of the programme.

Data architecture

Start with what is available. Design towards what would sharpen the evidence.

REVALUE-AU is designed as a staged data strategy. It can begin with transparent public and survey evidence, while building the relationships and specifications needed for stronger administrative and sector-level analyses.

HILDA

Worker-level wages, hours, employment and transitions for the pilot and design diagnostics.

Legal-economic platform

Structured decisions, occupations, awards, implementation dates, quantum and institutional mechanisms.

PLIDA and LFS

Prospective pathways to improve worker, employer and labour-market exposure measurement.

STP and sector data

Potentially sharper evidence on payroll adjustment, workforce stability, funding and service delivery.

For a host institution

A programme that leaves assets behind, not only papers.

In substance this is a programme about organisations and markets: how employers absorb a mandated re-pricing of their core workforce through wage structures, hours, staffing, retention, fees and publicly funded budgets, in sectors at the centre of Australia's care economy. It sits across labour economics, employment relations, human resource management and public policy.

A reusable national dataset

The legal-economic event platform outlives any single paper and can support work by others in the school.
